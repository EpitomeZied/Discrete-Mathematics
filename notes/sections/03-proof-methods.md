# S03 — Proof methods

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 03. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** the method is chosen from the *shape of the claim*, not from taste.

**Board — the four skeletons, written as templates to copy:**
- Direct: "Assume P. … Therefore Q. ∎"
- Contrapositive: "We prove ¬Q → ¬P. Assume ¬Q. …"
- Contradiction: "Suppose P ∧ ¬Q. … which is a contradiction. ∎"
- Cases: "Either … or …. Case 1: … Case 2: … In both cases, Q. ∎" — with *exhaustiveness* stated.

**I prove:** "if n² is even then n is even" — direct fails, contrapositive is three lines. Show the failure first; that is the whole point of the section.

**They prove:** √2 is irrational (contradiction), and "the sum of a rational and an irrational is irrational."

**Mistakes I expect:** contradiction attempted without ever stating the assumption; contrapositive with only one half negated; cases that do not cover everything; circularity.

**Close:** "Try direct. When the hypothesis gives you nothing to hold, negate the conclusion and see what that gives you."
