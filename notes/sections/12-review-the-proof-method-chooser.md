# S12 — Review: the proof-method chooser

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 12. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** given an unfamiliar claim, the *shape* of the claim tells you how to start. Starting correctly is most of the mark.

**Board — built live with them:**

| The claim looks like | Start with | First line on the page |
|---|---|---|
| "if P then Q", P is usable | direct | "Assume P." |
| "if P then Q", ¬Q is easier to use | contrapositive | "We prove ¬Q → ¬P." |
| "no such thing exists" / irrationality | contradiction | "Suppose such a thing exists." |
| "for all n ≥ n₀" | induction | "Base: n = n₀." |
| "A = B" for sets | double inclusion | "Let x ∈ A." |
| "for all x" but you suspect it is false | counterexample | one concrete x, fully checked |
| "how many …" | counting | "Ordered? Repetition?" |
| about divisibility or mod | definitions | "By definition, a = bk." |

**How the clinic runs:** no slides. I collect their requested topics a week ahead, bring the **top five misconceptions from my grading notes as plausible wrong answers**, and they vote on which is wrong and why. Then a timed past-paper question, followed by peer-marking with my rubric — grading someone else's proof is the fastest way to see your own gaps.

**Close:** "You are not graded on inspiration. You are graded on starting correctly and justifying every line."
