# S11 — Graphs and trees

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 11. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** the hard part is **modelling** — choosing what the vertices and edges *are*. The theorems are easy once the picture is right.

**Board:**
1. Vocabulary fast, on one drawn graph: degree, path, cycle, connected, simple, bipartite.
2. Handshake lemma proved by double counting (callback to S08), then its corollary: the number of odd-degree vertices is even.
3. Euler vs Hamiltonian, with the degree condition and the honest note that one has a clean criterion and the other does not.
4. Trees: `n - 1` edges, proved by induction (callback to S04); leaves; spanning trees.
5. Model one word problem as a graph *with them* — seating, scheduling, or map colouring.

**They prove:** a graph with all degrees ≥ 2 contains a cycle; and decide bipartiteness for two drawn graphs with justification.

**Mistakes I expect:** arguing from a single drawing as if it were general; confusing Euler and Hamiltonian conditions; assuming trees must be binary (a leftover from data structures); no drawing at all.
