# S07 — Relations

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 07. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** three properties, checked mechanically, decide everything — and the properties are about *all* pairs, so one counterexample kills one.

**Board:**
1. Reflexive / symmetric / transitive / antisymmetric, each with a tiny relation that has it and one that misses it by exactly one pair.
2. Congruence mod n verified as an equivalence relation, line by line — then its classes drawn as a partition. **Equivalence relation ⇔ partition** is the idea to land.
3. A partial order as a Hasse diagram; comparable vs incomparable.

**They prove:** verify a given relation on a small set is an equivalence relation, then list the classes; and find a relation that is symmetric and transitive but not reflexive.

**Mistakes I expect:** confusing symmetric with antisymmetric (they are not opposites); checking transitivity on one example and concluding it holds; missing that the empty relation is vacuously symmetric and transitive; classes that overlap.
