# S02 — Predicates and quantifiers

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 02. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** **order matters**, and negation flips every quantifier while it travels inward.

**Board:**
1. `∀x ∃y (y > x)` vs `∃y ∀x (y > x)` over integers — one true, one false. Same symbols, different claims. Make them say the difference in words.
2. Negation as a mechanical rule: `¬∀x P(x) ≡ ∃x ¬P(x)`; push the ¬ inward one quantifier at a time, out loud.
3. Translate three English statements into logic and back again — ambiguity in English is the lesson.
4. Vacuous truth: `∀x ∈ ∅, P(x)`.

**They prove/write:** negate "every student who studies passes every exam", then say what a counterexample must be — *one* student, *one* exam.

**Mistakes I expect:** swapping quantifier order freely; negating only the inner predicate; forgetting the domain; producing a counterexample to the wrong statement.

**Close:** "A counterexample is an ∃ statement. Know exactly what shape it must have before you go looking."
