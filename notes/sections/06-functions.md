# S06 — Functions

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 06. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** injective and surjective are two *independent* claims, each with its own fixed proof opening.

**Board:**
1. The templates: injective — "assume f(a) = f(b); show a = b." Surjective — "let y ∈ codomain; construct x with f(x) = y."
2. Same rule, three different domains/codomains, so the answer changes: `f(x) = x²` on ℝ→ℝ, ℝ→ℝ⁺, ℝ⁺→ℝ⁺. **The domain is part of the function** — this is the section's real content.
3. Composition and inverses; why the inverse exists exactly when f is a bijection.

**They prove:** decide and prove/disprove injectivity and surjectivity for three given functions, including one on ℤ.

**Mistakes I expect:** disproving injectivity with an assertion rather than two concrete inputs; "surjective because the formula looks onto"; ignoring the stated codomain; claiming an inverse for a non-bijection.
