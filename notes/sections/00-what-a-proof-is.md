# S00 — What a proof is

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 00. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** a proof is a *chain of justified steps aimed at a target* — an argument, not an answer.

**Board:**
1. Two "proofs" of the same true claim side by side: one complete, one a pile of true statements going nowhere. Ask which earns marks and why. This single comparison reframes the whole term.
2. What a justification can be: a definition, a hypothesis, a previously proved result, an algebraic law. Nothing else. Not "it's clear."
3. The anatomy: *what is given*, *what is claimed*, *what closes it*.

**Their turn:** write the definitions of even, odd, divides, rational — precisely, on paper, no phones. Then use "even" in a sentence *as the definition permits* (`n = 2k` for some integer k). Most proof failures all term are definition failures.

**Mistakes I expect:** "proving" by re-asserting the claim; treating examples as proof; starting from the conclusion and working back without saying so.

**Close:** "Every line you write, I get to ask *why is that allowed* — have the answer ready."
