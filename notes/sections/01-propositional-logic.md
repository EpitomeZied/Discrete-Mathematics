# S01 — Propositional logic

> [!NOTE]
> Discrete Math, section 01. Index: [Discrete Math Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Discrete Math Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Discrete Math Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** implication is not causation, not conversation, and it is *true when the premise is false*.

**Board:**
1. Truth table for → filled in with them, arguing about the two vacuous rows. Give a real example: "if it rains, I bring an umbrella" on a sunny day — no lie was told.
2. Converse / inverse / contrapositive from one statement, with only the contrapositive circled as equivalent.
3. De Morgan on paper, then negate a compound statement in three steps.
4. Equivalence by reasoning, not by table — show one rewrite chain.

**They prove:** show `p → q ≡ ¬p ∨ q` and use it to negate "if you study, you pass."

**Mistakes I expect:** believing the converse is equivalent; negating `p ∧ q` as `¬p ∧ ¬q`; missing that `p → q` is true when p is false; using ⇒ as a narrative arrow between unrelated lines.

**Close:** "Contrapositive is free and always equivalent. The converse is a different claim and owes its own proof."
